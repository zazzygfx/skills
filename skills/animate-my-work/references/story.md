# Story

## The beat sheet

Write the story in one sentence a stranger gets, such as "Box what you want changed, say it, and only that changes."
Every scene serves that sentence. Cut anything that doesn't.

Then write `SCRIPT.md`, one row per scene:

| Scene | Beats | On screen (every word) | Main move | Eye in → out | Camera | Sounds |
|-|-|-|-|-|-|-|

The beat grid is 128 BPM, so a beat is 0.46875 s and a bar is 1.875 s. Scene lengths are whole beats; prefer whole bars
where it fits. The film's total should be a whole number of bars, so the music ends on the end card.

## Pacing curve

The percentages are of the film's length.

| Part | Share | Rhythm |
|-|-|-|
| Hook | 0–15% | intrigue, one change every 2 bars; the music is still building |
| Build | to 40% | one change a bar; the drop lands on the logo or the first big reveal |
| Peak | to 65% | fastest: a change every beat or half bar, whips and swipes |
| Breathe | to 80% | a calmer scene on the music's break |
| Climax and outro | the rest | accelerate, then the end logo holds longest (2–3 s) on the final hit |

## Demo grammar (any scene that teaches an action)

Show the action literally, at a speed a stranger can follow:

1. **Arrive.** The canvas or UI is already there, and the camera eases in toward where the action will happen.
2. **Point.** The cursor travels in on a minimum-jerk path. It never teleports.
3. **Act.** A box drags out from its corner over about 0.8 s, locks with a tick, and its numbered badge pops. Text
   types letter by letter, with key clicks.
4. **Commit.** The cursor goes to the button, hovers for a third of a beat, and clicks: the button presses and a ring
   pulses.
5. **Work.** A render sweep plays across the canvas, about 1 s.
6. **Reveal.** The before/after divider sweeps over about 1.6 s. The "Before" chip and a time chip pop.
7. **Prove.** The divider slides off. The boxes come back on the result, pulsing on exactly what changed, and a chip
   says so.

Give a demo scene 12–16 beats. Spend the time on motion (the camera following the cursor, the type running, the wipe
moving), never on a frozen frame. Brand, step and recap scenes are quick, 4–8 beats. If a film runs long, cut the
quick scenes, never the demos.

## On-screen copy

- One idea per card. Titles of 2–5 words, sublines of 4–10 words.
- Real numbers and facts only. Research claims about a product from its official pages, and cite them in the hand-off.
- The step rail, labels and chips use the product's own vocabulary.
- Anything that states the length ("a 90-second guide") must match the cut.
- No credit card and no "made with". The last frame is the brand's end card.
